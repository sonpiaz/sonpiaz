A useful product can solve a real problem and still go unnoticed. Building and
distribution are different systems, and the second one does not appear just
because the first one works.

The common failure is not a lack of activity. Founders run ads, publish posts,
write for search, contact creators, and try to recruit a marketing team. Each
action may help, but disconnected actions do not become a channel. The founder
has to restart the work every week, so growth stops when their attention moves
back to the product.

## Distribution needs an owner

A repeatable channel answers a few basic questions:

- Who already has the trust of the people this product serves?
- Why would that person recommend it?
- How can both sides see what happened after the recommendation?
- When is a result confirmed?
- How does the partner get paid without asking for a manual update?

Those questions describe an operating system, not a campaign. The work starts
with finding the right partners, but it also needs onboarding, attribution,
review, communication, and payout. Leaving any one of those steps informal
makes the whole channel hard to trust.

## Trust comes before scale

Partners put their reputation behind a recommendation. They need a product they
understand, a clear agreement, and a record of the results. The business needs
the same record so it can review returns, invalid activity, and commissions
before a payout is final.

That balance matters more than recruiting a large list. A small group that knows
the product and can inspect the same attribution record is a stronger beginning
than a marketplace full of names with no reason to care.

## The system I wanted

I built [Affitor](/projects/affitor) around that sequence. A brand adds the
tracking, invites partners, records the path from click to sale, holds the
commission while the result is checked, and pays from the same record. The
product page keeps the current facts and evidence; the principle is simpler:
distribution becomes repeatable when both sides can see and trust the process.

The lesson is not that every company needs affiliate software. It is that growth
work needs a system with an owner. If distribution remains a pile of tasks, the
founder remains the integration between them.
